# Global Program Patterns

## Scope
Comparative synthesis of 12 analyzed programs:
Y Combinator, Techstars, 500 Global, Seedcamp, Station F, CcHUB, MEST Africa, Andela, Gebeya, iHub, Start-Up Chile, Startup SG.

## 1) Structural Archetypes

### A. Cohort Accelerator (3–4 months)
**Examples:** YC, Techstars, 500 Global  
**Pattern:** fixed intake windows, high-intensity execution cadence, mentor-heavy, demo day close.

### B. Pipeline / Platform Model (multi-program, rolling)
**Examples:** CcHUB, iHub, Station F, Startup SG umbrella  
**Pattern:** multiple entry points, thematic tracks, partner-delivered modules, variable timelines.

### C. Talent-to-Venture Builder
**Examples:** MEST, Andela-adjacent talent pipeline patterns, Gebeya skilling-to-deployment patterns  
**Pattern:** skills formation first, venture/market outcomes second, stronger operational support requirements.

### D. Public Policy Program
**Examples:** Start-Up Chile, Startup SG  
**Pattern:** grant or matching-fund instruments, milestone compliance, policy KPI orientation.

## 2) Recurring Program Design Signals
1. **Hybrid delivery is default** (in-person community + virtual flexibility).
2. **Mentorship is the core value driver**; curriculum quality alone is not enough.
3. **Selection quality predicts outcomes** more strongly than content breadth.
4. **Cohort peer effects matter** (founder accountability and network spillover).
5. **Post-program support separates top programs** (alumni capital access, market links, follow-on).

## 3) Duration & Cadence Benchmarks
- **3 months:** best for investor-readiness accelerators.
- **4 months:** allows deeper growth experiments (500-style).
- **6–12+ months:** needed for talent transformation, public programs, or sector-heavy ventures.
- **Cadence design:** weekly mentor touchpoints + biweekly progress gates + monthly milestone reviews.

## 4) Cohort Size Patterns
- **High-touch model:** 10–20 startups (higher quality coaching, stronger selection burden).
- **Mid-scale model:** 30–50 startups (requires standardized operating system).
- **Large-scale model:** 200+ (only viable with strong partner bench + brand gravity like YC).

## 5) Selection Patterns
- Consistent criteria across geographies:
  - Team quality and coachability
  - Market size and urgency
  - Evidence of traction/progress
  - Execution clarity
- Typical acceptance observed:
  - Elite private accelerators: ~1–4%
  - Public/thematic programs: often higher but less uniformly disclosed

## 6) Operating Model Patterns
- **Core team pods:** Program Lead, Ops Manager, Founder Success, Partnerships, Finance/Grants.
- **Partner layer:** mentors, investors, corporates, service credits, local ecosystem hosts.
- **Artifacts per cohort:** intake rubric, week-by-week calendar, milestone tracker, mentor logs, demo readiness checklist.

## 7) What This Means for IIH Product Design
The platform should natively support all 4 archetypes (accelerator, platform, talent pipeline, policy) through configurable templates, rather than one rigid “cohort-only” flow.

## 8) Minimum Reusable Pattern Library (for software templates)
1. Cohort setup template (duration, size, gates)
2. Selection workflow template (application -> scoring -> panel -> final)
3. Mentor management template (matching, scheduling, notes)
4. Milestone governance template (weekly/biweekly/monthly checkpoints)
5. Graduation and post-program template (demo day, alumni follow-up)
